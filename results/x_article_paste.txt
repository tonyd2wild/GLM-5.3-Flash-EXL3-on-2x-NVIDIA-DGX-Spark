NVFP4 vs EXL3 on DGX Spark: same model, same boxes, same minute

GLM-5.3-Flash, two 4-bit quants, two 2-node lanes of DGX Spark, benched simultaneously from c1 to c6. Time to first token, prefill, decode, wall-to-wall, context, KV pool, boot time, and a 12-item reasoning battery with thinking off and on.

[IMAGE: card_nvfp4_vs_exl3.png]


THE SHORT VERSION

Split decision.

• NVFP4 decodes faster. 64.0 vs 61.5 tok/s single stream (+4%), +14 to 30% per stream at c2 to c5, higher aggregate at 5 of 6 concurrency levels, faster wall-to-wall through c5.

• EXL3 answers first. Warm time to first token 0.52 s vs 1.54 s at c1 and 1.0 s vs 4.6 s at c6. Prefill 3,099 vs 1,055 tok/s (2.9x). 1M context and a 1.40M-token KV pool vs 256K and 295K on the same two boxes. Boots to serving in 13 min vs 23.

• Quality is a tie. 12-item reasoning battery: 11/12 vs 11/12 with thinking off (same miss), 12/12 vs 12/12 with thinking on. The quant did not change how smart the model is.

• Our first run said EXL3 won everything. It was wrong. Two of the four Sparks were clock-capped at roughly 700 MHz after a reboot. We caught it, restarted all four, and ran the entire test again. Every number here is from the second run.


WHAT WE SERVED

Lane 1, NVFP4 (Reddie + Spark4)
• Weights: RedHatAI/GLM-5.3-Flash-NVFP4
• Runtime: vLLM, image ghcr.io/tonyd2wild/vllm-glm53-flash:sm121-v11-dflash2, the published 2-Spark recipe run verbatim
• Context: 262,144. KV pool: 295,230 tokens. KV dtype fp8_e4m3, MoE backend marlin
• Speculative decode: DFlash2 k=7. Graphs: enforce-eager. max-num-seqs 6

Lane 2, EXL3 (Bluey + Asusi)
• Weights: brandonmusic/GLM-5.3-Flash-tr3-4bpw (EXL3 / TR3 4bpw)
• Runtime: vLLM + exllamav3 built for sm_121a, Reederey87's 2-Spark kit
• Context: 1,048,576. KV pool: 1,396,551 tokens. KV dtype fp8 (fp8_ds_mla), MoE exl3_moe trellis
• Speculative decode: DFlash2 k=7. Graphs: enforce-eager. max-num-seqs 4 (this caps c5 and c6; it is config, not the quant)

Hardware: 4x NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10, sm_121a, 128 GB unified memory), ConnectX-7 RoCE v2 fabric, two boxes per lane, tensor parallel 2, vLLM multiprocess executor. Bench client on a Mac mini over Tailscale.


HOW WE MEASURED

• Isolation. Before measuring, every other consumer was moved off both lanes: the relay our agents use was parked on a different model, the coding-latency monitor that sends real probe completions was paused, and the three supervisor agents that default to this model were moved to a 27B on another box. We then proved it from each head's access log: NVFP4 head 128 chat requests in the window, all from the bench client; EXL3 head 129 from the bench client, and the only other traffic in the 30-minute log window was the kit's own post-serve warm-up burst of 15 requests, fifteen minutes before the tests began. Zero outside requests on either lane during the test.

• Simultaneity. Both lanes were benched in parallel, same minute, same state. They share no GPUs, memory, or NCCL group.

• Warm-up. Both engines JIT-compile kernels per request shape. We warmed each lane with 2x c1 and 1x c6 before measuring. A cold lane is not the lane's speed: EXL3's first-ever long prefill ran at 430 tok/s, warm it runs at 3,099.

• Clocks. Healthy GB10 decode sits near 2,180 MHz at 95% utilization. After a reboot, two nodes were pinned at 611 to 728 MHz under load. We checked all four under a real request before this run: 2,171 to 2,190 MHz.

• Settings. Non-streaming, temperature 0, thinking off for the speed tests, roughly 300-token answers. Sweep: 3 rounds per concurrency level, medians. Single-stream: 5 rounds. Prefill: a 1,623-token prompt, warm median of the last 3 of 6.

CONTEXT, KV POOL, BOOT

• Context that booted: NVFP4 262,144. EXL3 1,048,576 (4x).
• KV pool: NVFP4 295,230 tokens. EXL3 1,396,551 tokens (4.7x).
• Boot, launch command to first healthy response: NVFP4 23 min (worker loads its weights over NFS from the head). EXL3 13 min (local weights on both nodes, warm trellis JIT cache).

The NVFP4 recipe cannot boot at 1M context on these two boxes; there is not enough room left for the KV pool. We learned that the hard way and it is why the recipe pins 256K.


QUALITY: DOES THE QUANT CHANGE HOW SMART IT IS?

Probe. Same prompt to both, thinking off, temperature 0: write top-k-frequent in O(n log k) with an explanation and an edge case, plus the bat-and-ball trap. Both correct, both answered $0.05 with a check line.

Battery. Twelve auto-graded items with checkable final answers: three multi-step math word problems, three logic puzzles, two code items (predict the output, fix the one-line bug), a leap-year rule, the bat-and-ball trap, and two strict-format tasks (exact JSON, reversed string). Identical prompts to both lanes, temperature 0, run twice: thinking off (direct answer) and thinking on (the model reasons first).

Accuracy (NVFP4 vs EXL3)
• Thinking off: 11/12 vs 11/12. Same single miss on both: reversing the word "benchmark".
• Thinking on: 12/12 vs 12/12. Every item finished naturally, none hit the token limit.
• By category, thinking off: math 3/3 vs 3/3, logic 3/3 vs 3/3, code 2/2 vs 2/2, knowledge 1/1 vs 1/1, reasoning 1/1 vs 1/1, format 1/2 vs 1/2.
• Reasoning trace length, thinking on: 341 chars average for NVFP4, 333 for EXL3.

The traces themselves, same item, both lanes, thinking on. The clock-hands angle at 3:15:

NVFP4: "At 3:15, the minute hand is at 15 minutes, which is at the 3 position (90 degrees from 12). The hour hand: at 3:00 it's at 90 degrees. In 15 minutes, it moves 15 x 0.5 = 7.5 degrees. So hour hand is at 97.5 degrees. Minute hand at 15 minutes: 15 x 6 = 90 degrees. Angle between: 97.5 - 90 = 7.5 degrees."

EXL3: "At 3:15, the minute hand is at 15 minutes, which is at the 3 position (90 degrees from 12). The hour hand at 3:15 has moved past 3. At 3:00 it's at 90 degrees. Each minute, the hour hand moves 0.5 degrees. So at 3:15, hour hand is at 90 + 7.5 = 97.5 degrees. Minute hand at 15 minutes: 15 x 6 = 90 degrees. Angle between them: 97.5 - 90 = 7.5 degrees."

Published KLD-vs-FP16 figures put EXL3/TR3 4bpw near 0.025 (tying FP8) and NVFP4 near 0.060, so a gap is expected to exist in the output distribution. On a twelve-item task battery it did not show. Twelve items is a probe of intelligence, not a benchmark suite; treat any single-item difference as noise unless it repeats. One honest footnote: temperature-0 answers were not batch-invariant on either engine. The reversed-string item passed on both lanes in one run and failed on both in the next, with identical prompts.


WHAT WE GOT WRONG THE FIRST TIME

Our first pass, published to nobody, had EXL3 winning every metric: 1.7x single stream, 5.7x prefill, NVFP4 swinging ±16% run to run. The tell was that NVFP4's numbers did not match its own recipe's published peak. Two of the four Sparks had come out of a reboot with their GPU clocks pinned between 611 and 728 MHz under load, against a healthy 2,180. Restart, verify clocks under a real request on all four nodes, run everything again. NVFP4 went from 35.9 to 64.0 tok/s single stream and from ±16% to ±1.5%. Check clocks before you trust any number on this hardware.


WHICH ONE TO RUN

• Interactive agents, long context, many concurrent sessions: EXL3. The first token lands 3 to 4x sooner under load, the KV pool is 4.7x larger, and 1M context boots on the same two boxes.
• Batch generation, short prompts, throughput per box: NVFP4. It decodes 4% faster alone and 14 to 30% faster per stream at moderate concurrency.
• Both run DFlash2 speculative decoding, both answer the same questions the same way. Pick on latency profile and context, not on quality.


PINS THAT MATTER

• NVFP4: run the published 2-Spark recipe verbatim. 256K context, worker first, head 25 s later. vm.swappiness=0 resets on reboot. Poll /health, never /v1/models.
• EXL3 kit: count_shards needs find -L on a symlinked HF cache. chown the vLLM cache dir on both nodes. It ships bound to loopback; set --host 0.0.0.0. The worker needs the full 164 GiB of weights on local disk.
• Both: roughly 91 GiB of weights per 121 GiB usable node. Drop page caches on every node before every launch.
• Both: kernels JIT-compile per request shape. Warm up before measuring and never bench a cold lane.
• Both: check GPU clocks under load after any reboot.
